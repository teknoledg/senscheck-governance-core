## Allowed without asking

- **Saying what it is.** "Built on SensCheck Governance — Core", "compatible with SensCheck", "uses the SensCheck CLI", in plain text, in docs, articles, talks and product pages.
- **Linking** to this repository or the official site.
- **Unmodified copies** distributed under the same name, with the LICENSE and NOTICE files intact.
- **Accurate descriptions** of your own project's relationship ("a fork of SensCheck Governance — Core").

## Not allowed without written permission

- **Naming a fork, product, company, package or domain "SensCheck"**, or anything confusingly similar (for example in an npm scope, a GitHub organisation, a hosted service or a browser extension).
- **Using the logo** as your own product's logo, or in a way that implies endorsement or official status.
- **Distributing a modified version under the SensCheck name.** If you change the behaviour, rename it. Governance code that claims the SensCheck name must be the real one; users rely on its fail-closed guarantees.
- **Implying certification, partnership or endorsement** by TEKNOLED-G LIMITED, MMRY, SensAffect or Akita that does not exist.
- **Marketing a modified or competing hosted service as "SensCheck Platform"** or any other official name.

## Why this matters for a governance tool

People decide whether to trust an action based on what a governance layer says. A modified build that keeps the name but drops a check could mislead them. Keeping the name tied to the official, verifiable releases protects users, not just us. See [docs/RELEASING.md](docs/RELEASING.md) for how to verify an official release.
